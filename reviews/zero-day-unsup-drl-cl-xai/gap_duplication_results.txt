Overall: no paper duplicates any of the four gaps.

======================================================================
G1. XAI decides which pseudo-labels are used
======================================================================
Definition : Explanations decide whether an automatically labelled zero-day sample may enter
             continual retraining.
Status     : OPEN
Closest    : - Afzaal et al. 2026 (arXiv 2605.26166): keeps a pseudo-label only if confidence >= 0.85
               and the encoder and decoder agree.
             - Guo et al. 2025 (Future Internet, Q2): filters pseudo-labels by prediction entropy
               and agreement.
             - SOUL 2024 and RSST-NIDS 2026 (arXiv): filter pseudo-labels by confidence.
             - UNAD+ 2026 (arXiv): a person checks a subset of pseudo-labels; LIME is used only
               to explain results afterwards.
             - CADE 2021 (USENIX Security): explains drifting samples to help analysts label them.
Still new  : No paper uses explanations to accept or reject pseudo-labels. Every existing filter
             uses confidence, model agreement or a person.
Evidence   : - AOC-IDS without a filter: accuracy collapses from 90% to 75% over 50 batches
               (reported by Afzaal et al.).
             - GARDIAN on UGRansome (our run): pseudo-labels were 82.5% correct, and fine-tuning
               on them made the classifier worse (0.829 -> 0.787 on new families).
Baseline   : The PseudoFilter confidence filter from Afzaal et al.
Strength   : Strong. Core contribution.

======================================================================
G2. DRL agent that uses explanations to decide retraining
======================================================================
Definition : A DRL agent uses explanation signals (in its state or reward) to decide when and how
             much to retrain a detector, with skip or rollback actions.
Status     : PARTIALLY ADDRESSED
Closest    : - DeepXplain 2026 (arXiv, under review at GLOBECOM 2026): explanations shape DRL
               training, but the agent chooses APT response actions, not retraining.
             - C-MADF 2026 (arXiv): an explainability score gates autonomous actions.
             - Drago et al. 2026 (ITASEC & SERICS, CEUR): changes in SHAP feature importance
               trigger adaptation, without RL.
             - GARDIAN 2026 (JISA): a DRL agent controls retraining, without XAI.
Still new  : The combination: a DRL agent that decides when to retrain a NIDS, uses explanation
             shift as input, and can skip or roll back updates.
Positioning: Must be compared explicitly against DeepXplain, C-MADF and Drago et al.
Strength   : Medium. Good extension, not a standalone contribution.

======================================================================
G3. Explaining continual updates
======================================================================
Definition : Explanations that show how the detector changes across updates (forgetting, effect
             of drift).
Status     : PARTIALLY ADDRESSED
Closest    : - Drago et al. 2026: explains the impact of drift and how model decisions evolve.
             - CADE 2021: detects and explains drifting samples.
             - CMC 2023 (Computers, Materials & Continua): SHAP-based drift detection without
               labels.
             - Outside security: "Explain to Not Forget" (arXiv 2205.01929) and the 2025 WIREs
               review "XAI-Guided Continual Learning" use XAI against forgetting.
Still new  : Only explaining the forgetting caused by each update (replay or generated samples)
             inside a NIDS.
Strength   : Weak as a standalone contribution. Better used as an analysis section.

======================================================================
G4. Feature encoding vs zero-day detection
======================================================================
Definition : How categorical feature encoding affects whether unsupervised methods can detect
             zero-day traffic.
Status     : OPEN (narrow)
Closest    : None. ACORN-IDS and AOC-IDS (one-hot) and ThreatFormer-IDS (embeddings) choose an
             encoding without studying it; Alkasassbeh et al. 2025 use integer (label) encoding.
Still new  : No study of how categorical encoding changes zero-day detectability.
Evidence   : Our UGRansome test: autoencoder AUC 0.49 with integer codes vs 0.94 with one-hot.
Strength   : Supporting finding. Too narrow to stand alone.
